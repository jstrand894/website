About me

Currently, I am a PhD student in Ecology & Environmental Science at Montana State University in Bozeman. I study how insects can be used to manage invasive plants. I am happiest when I am outside, working with insects, and digging into statistics — a combination that took me a few detours to find.

Below is the short version of how I got here.

Mosquitoes and wetlands

At the Unviersity of Minnesota, I thought I wanted to be a geneticist. I loved dinosaurs as a kid (still do!), and so of course combining dinosaurs and genetics had an obvious allure. However, afer spending hours pipetting in labs with few windows and limited social contact, I decided to pivot. As a junior, I switched to a general biology degree with a minor in marine biology. I still wasn't sure what I wanted my career to be, but at least I was going in the right direction. After graduation, I worked for the Metropolitan Mosquito Control District in Oakdale, MN. I was tasked with driving around to various freshwater sources to look for mosquito larvae and treat them with larvicide. Admittedly, I spent more time looking for cool places and adding to my insect collection than I did on the job itself.

A cubicle at 3M

After the seasonal job came to an end, I took a job at 3M (think scotch tape and post-it notes) managing their database systems. Among them included a propietary chemical data management system (CDMS), along with SAP and a whole lot of Excel. I stayed for three years and came to realize that I did not want a career as a regulatory analyst. I had a degree in biology, after all, and this job wasn't what I dreamed of doing.

Eventually I decided that I wanted to change careers and learn more about insects and ecology.

Applying to graduate school

I started applying to graduate programs in 2020 and 2021, which turned out to be a time when everybody else had the same idea. I heard back from about 10% of the position openings I applied to, and half of the replies were some version of “I received over 100 applicants, I am overwhelmed. Thank you for applying.”

Eventually, I caught a break. An email from Dr. David Weaver at Montana State University.

Montana and the wheat stem sawfly

I started a master’s degree with Dr. Weaver in 2021. The project was a great fit as it combined my passions: insects, statistics, and the outdoors. I studied the wheat stem sawfly, a pest of wheat, and how smooth brome and native parasitoid wasps shape it. You can learn more about my project in my talks and presentations tab as well as my publications.

Continuing to a PhD

After my master’s, I wanted to continue to use insects to solve real-world challenges. In 2025, I began a PhD with Dr. Sharlene Sing and Dr. Bob Peterson, studying the biological control of Dalmatian and yellow toadflax -- invasive weeds in the American West.

I now study weevils that we raise and release to attack invasive toadflax and we work to understand their efficacy and resilience in difference ecosystem and climate scenarios.

Outside of research

When I am not in the field or at my desk, you will usually find me in the mountains around Bozeman. I spend a lot of time trail running, and I’ve completed two 100 mile trail races, with a 200-miler scheduled for the summer of 2027. I also help lead the LRES Graduate Student Organization at MSU.

Let’s connect

If you want to talk about biological control, chemical ecology, or trails around Bozeman, I would love to hear from you.
